## Why doesn't the theory cover everything?
##

Because it isn't trying to.

Rust has a large surface area, and this course doesn't attempt to cover all of it.
Each lesson covers _just enough_ to keep you moving, one step at a time.

So yes, now and then an exercise will ask for something the theory above it didn't spell out.
That is intentional, not an oversight.
Looking things up is part of the work here, the same way it's part of the work in a real project.

It isn't a sign that you're behind, either.
Experienced Rust developers spend a good part of their day in the standard library documentation.

## Where to look

- **The standard library docs**, at [doc.rust-lang.org/std](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/), searchable and full of
  examples. You can also put the caret on any type or method in the editor and invoke **Quick Documentation** to read
  the same docs without leaving the IDE.
- **The Rust Book**, at [doc.rust-lang.org/book](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/), a second and longer explanation of
  most concepts we touch on here. A different angle on the same idea is often what makes it click.
- **A web search.** Whatever confuses you has confused thousands of people before you, and they wrote it down.
- **An AI assistant.** See the next question.
